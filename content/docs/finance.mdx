**Finance** is in the dashboard (it is switched on per business). Everything is worked out from what each invoice recorded when it was issued: its subtotal, discount, tax by rate and total. A report never recomputes, so it cannot disagree with an invoice.

- A **period** is the issue date. Draft invoices are not sales. A voided invoice is shown in the register, marked, and left out of the totals.
- Each **currency** is reported on its own. Dollars and riel are never added together.
- Dates are in your viewer's zone, so a day means your day.

## Analytics

Issued, paid, outstanding and overdue amounts; monthly recurring revenue from active subscriptions; money paid over time; who owes you, by how many days late (not due, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+); and the coupons used.

## Tax

Per currency and rate: the invoices, the taxable amount and the tax charged. Rates are grouped by what was charged (name, percentage, included or not), so a rate you edited mid-period shows as the two it was. It also shows sales with no tax and voided invoices, and your registration number.

## Reports (CSV)

| Report | One row per | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax invoice register | issued invoice | A tax filing: number, date, customer and their tax ID, your tax ID, tax by rate (up to three, then a count), total, status |
| Sales ledger | invoice line | Revenue by line: description, quantity, amount, discount, tax |
| Receivables aging | open invoice | Collections: due date, days late, bucket |
| Payments reconciliation | paid invoice | The bank: receipt, method, bank reference, settled date |

Amounts are plain decimals in the invoice's currency. Cells that could run as a formula in a spreadsheet are escaped. Each download is recorded in the [audit log](/docs/production), and a file holds at most 100,000 rows: narrow the period for more.

## What it is not

These are reports of your invoices, with the data tax authorities ask for (sequential numbers, buyer and seller tax IDs, tax by rate). They do not file anything, and whether a format meets your country's rules is for you and your accountant to confirm.
